Generating Through the Noise: Progressive Denoising Diffusion for
Infrared Small Target Detection
First Author, Member, IEEE, and Second Author, Member, IEEE
Abstract—Infrared small target detection (IRSTD) aims to
accurately identify dim and tiny targets in complex infrared
scenes. However, weak target responses, low signal-to-noise ratios,
and severe background interference make reliable target local-
ization challenging, often resulting in missed detections and false
alarms. Existing methods typically rely on deterministic feature
learning to directly predict target masks, limiting the ability
to progressively refine ambiguous target representations. To
address these issues, we propose Progressive Denoising Diffusion
(PDDiff), a conditional diffusion framework that reformulates
IRSTD as a progressive generative denoising process. Specifically,
a feature extractor is employed to capture discriminative infrared
representations, followed by a conditional generator that con-
structs target guidance for subsequent denoising. Furthermore,
Memory-guided Spectral Alignmen (MSA) are introduced to
selectively refine conditional representations through frequency-
domain memory selection, enabling adaptive guidance through-
out the denoising process. By injecting the refined conditional
information into the denoising network, PDDiff iteratively re-
constructs accurate target masks from noisy representations.
In addition, customized training and sampling strategies are
developed to improve sparse target structure reconstruction
and exploit intermediate predictions throughout the diffusion
process, thereby mitigating structural errors and localization
instability. Extensive experiments on two benchmark IRSTD
datasets demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed PDDiff
in terms of detection accuracy and computational efficiency. The
code will be published at https://github.com/jzw0707/PDDiff.

II. RELATED WORK
A. Infrared small target detection methods
Existing IRSTD methods can be broadly divided into tradi-
tional and deep learning-based approaches. Traditional meth-
ods, including Tophat [19], MPCN [20], PSTNN [21], and IPI
[22], mainly rely on handcrafted priors, sparse representations,
and low-rank tensor decomposition. However, their perfor-
mance is often limited by complex background clutter and
weak target responses. Deep learning methods have achieved
significant progress through various architectures. CNN-based
methods, such as ACM [23] and DNANet [11], employ contex-
tual modeling and attention mechanisms to enhance target rep-
resentations. Transformer-based methods, such as RKformer
[24] and BMPTransNet [25], explore hybrid architectures for
long-range dependency modeling. Frequency-aware methods,
including Dim2Clear [26] and GSFANet [27], exploit spatial-
frequency interactions to improve feature discrimination. More
recently, mamba-based methods, such as MiMISTD [28] and
SAMamba [29], leverage Mamba architectures for efficient
feature modeling, while FAMENet [30] investigate expert-
based modeling and multimodal guidance, respectively.
However, these IRSTD methods adopt a single-step predic-
tion paradigm, directly mapping ambiguous target responses
to segmentation masks without explicit error correction, often
leading to missed detections and false alarms. In contrast,
diffusion models offer a progressive denoising mechanism
that enables iterative refinement of uncertain predictions under
complex background clutter. Nevertheless, directly applying
conventional diffusion models to IRSTD remains challenging.
During training, conventional Gaussian perturbations overlook
the sparse structure of target masks, while during sampling,
relying solely on the final prediction fails to exploit useful
information from intermediate denoising steps. To address
these limitations, we introduce SMSP and TPA for training

3
and sampling, respectively. SMSP enhances structure-aware
denoising of sparse targets, while TPA aggregates intermediate
predictions to improve detection accuracy.
B. Diffusion Models
Diffusion models have attracted increasing attention due to
their remarkable generative capabilities and ability to model
complex data distributions through iterative denoising. Early
studies, such as DDPM [31] and latent diffusion models [32],
demonstrated their effectiveness in high-quality image gen-
eration. Subsequently, diffusion models have been extended
to low-level vision tasks, including image super-resolution
and restoration. Representative methods, such as SR3 [33]
and DiffIR [34], progressively reconstruct images from noisy
representations through conditional denoising. More recently,
diffusion models have shown considerable potential in high-
level vision tasks. For example, DiffusionDet [35] formulates
object detection as a denoising process over bounding boxes,
while DiMSOD [36] employs diffusion to generate accurate
saliency masks for multimodal salient object detection. Despite
the growing success of diffusion models in various vision
tasks, recent studies have explored their application to IRSTD.
Du et al. [37] proposed ISTD-diff, which formulates IRSTD
as a conditional mask generation process through iterative
denoising. Li et al. [38] introduced posterior distribution
modeling with wavelet-domain isolation to suppress infrared
noise interference. Zhang et al. [39] further proposed DEDM-
Net, which employs dual-path diffusion modeling for mask
generation.
Given the progressive denoising nature of diffusion models,
we argue that preserving sparse target structures during train-
ing and exploiting intermediate predictions during sampling
are essential for IRSTD. However, conventional diffusion
models [40], [41] primarily rely on Gaussian perturbations,
which fail to explicitly account for the sparse structures
of target masks, limiting their ability to correct structural
errors during mask generation. Moreover, relying solely on
the final denoising output overlooks valuable information from
intermediate predictions, potentially leading to inaccurate seg-
mentation. To address these limitations, we introduce SMSP
to simulate structural deviations in sparse target masks and
enhance structure reconstruction during training, while TPA
adaptively aggregates intermediate predictions to improve the
robustness of generated masks during sampling.

D. Denoising Network
As illustrated in Fig. 3, the denoising network adopts a four-
level encoder-decoder architecture to progressively reconstruct
target masks from noisy representations. The encoder con-
sists of four convolutional stages, where timestep embeddings
are incorporated to adapt feature extraction across different
noise levels. To provide target-sensitive guidance, Enhance-
ment blocks are inserted after the first three encoder stages
to refine intermediate features using conditional representa-
tions St
i modulated by the diffusion timestep. Specifically,
each Enhancement block employs three parallel convolutional
branches with kernel sizes of 1, 3, and 5, followed by spatial
attention (SA) to capture complementary spatial responses.
The resulting attention maps are aggregated to modulate the
encoder features through residual enhancement. The deepest
encoder features are further utilized by MSA to generate
frequency-guided conditional information Mct. The decoder
then integrates Mct to reconstruct the predicted target mask
ˆxt, which is used for subsequent denoising steps.
As for loss function, we employ a hybrid loss function
combining Focal loss and IoU loss. The overall loss function
is formulated as:
L = λ1Lfocal + λ2Liou,
(16)
where λ1 and λ2 are the weighting coefficients for Focal
loss and IoU loss, empirically set to 2 and 5, respectively.
The combined objective enhances the discrimination of sparse
targets while improving the spatial consistency of predicted
masks.
E. Training Strategy
Sparse Mask Structure Perturbation (SMSP). Conven-
tional diffusion models typically corrupt ground-truth masks
through Gaussian noise injection, which primarily introduces
pixel-level disturbances without explicitly modeling structural
errors in target predictions. Consequently, the denoising net-
work may rely excessively on noisy mask structures, limiting
its ability to correct inaccurate predictions during sampling,
particularly for sparse infrared targets. To address this issue,
we introduce SMSP to improve the robustness of target mask
reconstruction. Specifically, given a ground-truth mask x0,
SMSP randomly perturbs its target structures through local
contour deformation, erosion, dilation, and spatial shifting,
generating a structurally corrupted mask ex0. This process is
formulated as:

IV. EXPERIMENTS AND RESULTS
A. Experimental Setup
Experiments are conducted on two public benchmarks,
SIRST-UAVB [59] and SIRST4 [60], containing 3,000 and
3,352 infrared images, respectively. SIRST4 integrates several
widely used datasets, including SIRSTv2, NUDT-SIRST, and
IRSTD-1K, covering diverse backgrounds and target scales.
SIRST-UAVB mainly contains UAV-based urban scenes char-
acterized by complex clutter. The dataset split follows the
default setting. For evaluation, we adopt standard metrics
widely used in IRSTD, including intersection over union
(IoU), F1 measure, probability of detection (Pd), and false
alarm rate (Fa).

We implement our PDDiff using PyTorch on two NVIDIA

injection to enhance structure denoising. Random cropping,
intensity jittering, and flipping are further applied for data
augmentation. The network is optimized using a combination
of Focal loss and IoU loss, with weighting coefficients λ1 = 2
and λ2 = 5, respectively. During sampling, TPA aggregates
intermediate predictions across denoising steps to improve
detection robustness.
B. Compared with SOTA methods
1) Quantitative Results: As reported in Table I, PDDiff

achieves the lowest Fa of 1.87, PDDiff maintains a relatively
low Fa of 2.53 while delivering superior detection accuracy.
On SIRST4, PDDiff consistently achieves the best IoU, Pd,
and F1 of 79.45%, 97.14%, and 88.55%, respectively. Com-
pared with IRPNet, the second-best method in IoU, PDDiff
improves IoU by 1.89%. It also exceeds UIUNet by 0.30%
in Pd and HDNet by 1.27% in F1. Furthermore, compared
with IRSTD-Diff, PDDiff improves IoU and Pd by 4.77% and
3.78%, respectively. Overall, the results on the two datasets
further demonstrate the effectiveness of diffusion progressive
denoising for IRSTD, which is well suited to the noise-
sensitive characteristics of infrared imaging, enabling gradual
suppression of background clutter and imaging noise while
improving the discrimination of weak targets from target-like
interference.

1) Integrated Framework.: Table II reports the ablation

relevant memory features through Top-K frequency similarity
and performing alignment, MSA enhances target discrimina-
tion while suppressing irrelevant background responses.

fectiveness of the proposed components. The baseline exhibits
widespread background activations, indicating severe interfer-
ence from imaging scenes. By introducing diffusion denoising
and DMSP, background responses are gradually suppressed
while weak target features become more distinguishable. With
MCA, target activations become more concentrated through
timestep-aware conditional modulation. MSA further enhances
target-related responses through frequency-guided memory
selection and alignment, reducing residual background inter-
ference. These results demonstrate that the proposed modules
provide discriminative conditional guidance for progressive
denoising, enabling iterative refinement of uncertain target
predictions and more reliable target localization.

larger backbones introduce substantially more parameters.

computational efficiency.

target-preserving spatial filtering and more robust frequency-

challenging infrared scenes.
V. CONCLUSION
In this paper, we presented Progressive Denoising Diffusion
(PDDiff), a conditional diffusion framework for infrared small
target detection. PDDiff establishes a progressive generative
denoising paradigm to address ambiguous target responses and
background interference in conventional single-step prediction
methods. In the conditional generation stage, DMSP enhances
target-sensitive spatial representations through dynamic mul-
tiscale projection, while MSA selectively retrieves relevant
memory features through frequency-domain similarity and
phase alignment to provide discriminative conditional guid-
ance. Furthermore, SMSP and TPA are introduced to improve
sparse target structure learning during training and interme-
diate prediction aggregation during sampling, respectively.
Extensive experiments on benchmark datasets demonstrate the
effectiveness of PDDiff in terms of detection accuracy, noise
robustness, and false-alarm suppression. Ablation studies fur-
ther validate the contributions of the proposed components and
strategies. These results indicate that progressive generative
denoising provides an effective approach for reliable infrared
small target detection. Future work will explore adaptive noise
modeling and efficient sampling strategies to further improve
detection robustness and computational efficiency.
